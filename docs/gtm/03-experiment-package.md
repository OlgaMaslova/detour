## 1. Decision summary and strict factual guardrails

### Decision

Run a first, bounded acquisition experiment around one demonstrable product truth:

> **The map of exceptional tables already narrowed to a credible, traceable city shortlist.**

Use San Francisco first. An operator can show the exact public count, map, selected-place proof, recognition source/year where displayed, and official-guide link in a short screen-led sequence. If that journey produces interpretable, qualified city-page use—not merely reach—Paris may follow as a high-threshold Michelin-led sequence test, then Madrid as a visible multi-source sequence test. [S1][S2]

### Verified public facts that may be used

- Detour publicly presents finite city selections: **San Francisco 25, Paris 29, Madrid 43**. [S1]
- City pages are map-led and expose the complete selection, with Search, destination, Refine, and Show nearby controls observed in the audit. [S1]
- Selected places can show “Why it’s here,” recognition level, named source, year, full address, and an Official guide link. [S1]
- In San Francisco, the observed source mix includes Michelin 3-, 2-, and 1-star restaurants, Bib Gourmand entries, and **Tony’s Pizza Napoletana — No. 3, 50 Top Pizza USA 2025**. [S1]
- The audited Benu detail showed **3 Stars, Michelin Guide 2026**, its full address, and an Official guide link. [S1]
- Paris showed 29 Michelin two- and three-star tables; the audited Alléno Paris au Pavillon Ledoyen detail showed **3 Stars, Michelin Guide 2026**, its address, and an Official guide link. [S1]
- Madrid’s audited 43-place selection includes Michelin and Guía Repsol, with some multi-source recognition; DiverXO was the visible example. [S1]
- Public community copy says three independent member recommendations can publish a place, while identities, personal notes, and introductions remain private. [S1]
- The public member route offers Sign in and Use an invitation. The anonymous audit did **not** reveal a clear route for a non-member to request or obtain an invitation. [S1]

### Strict guardrails

Every operator, editor, interviewer, and approver must apply these rules:

1. **No universal personal-review claim.** Do not say Detour, its team, or its members personally visited, tested, reviewed, or recommend every listed place.
2. **No booking claim.** Do not call Detour a booking, reservation, availability, or transaction product. No public booking or availability flow was observed. [S1]
3. **No save/share claim.** Do not promise public saving, list sharing, collaboration, or social-feed behavior. No public save or share control was observed. [S1]
4. **No universal member-recommendation claim.** Do not imply every public listing has member recommendations or exposes private member judgment.
5. **No unsupported invitation availability.** Do not say “request an invite,” “join the waitlist,” “invites available,” or “we’ll invite you” unless an approved, verified route exists at launch. The current public flow does not show a clear invite-request path. [S1]
6. **No “best restaurants” superlative.** Use “selected,” “recognized,” “credible,” “traceable,” “finite,” or “shortlist”; do not claim universal superiority or personal fit.
7. **No source overgeneralization.** Cite only the sources visibly present for the city or place being shown. Paris should not be described as multi-source based on this audit. [S1]
8. **No endorsement implication.** Named guides, publishers, organizations, creators, and target candidates have not endorsed or partnered with Detour unless later documented with explicit permission.
9. **No private disclosure.** Never expose member identities, notes, introductions, invitation codes, recipient details, or private activity in public content or research materials.
10. **No unapproved external action.** This package authorizes no post, recruitment, interview, invitation send, direct message, email, or partner approach.

## 3. Referral and invitation-flow specification

**Purpose:** Define a careful, measurable member-led invitation experiment around the existing limit of **three unclaimed personal invitations per member**. This is a process/copy specification only; it proposes no incentive and requires no code change as part of this package.

### A. Verified existing behavior versus recommendations

#### Verified existing behavior

- Membership is invitation-based; a recipient can use a personal invitation code on the public member route.
- Each member may have at most **three unclaimed personal invitations at one time**.
- An invitation is personal and one-time.
- Current semantics are **code creation and redemption**, not a verified built-in email or messaging delivery system:
  - the member creates a server-generated invitation code;
  - that created, unclaimed code occupies one of the three slots;
  - a successful redemption marks it claimed, activates the recipient’s membership, and frees one slot for the issuer;
  - the current member surface shows unclaimed codes and an “invitations left” count, and holds/disables new-code creation when no slot remains.
- The anonymous audit did not verify a public invite-request route, authenticated distribution behavior, or member comprehension. [S1]

#### Copy/process recommendations

- Frame an invitation as a personal introduction to a private community, not as scarcity theater or a reward.
- Ask members to invite only someone they know and believe would value a proof-led city shortlist and respect private participation.
- Provide a short, accurate recipient message; do not expose private member activity or imply that the recipient has been publicly endorsed.
- Keep “create code,” “share code,” and “redeem code” conceptually separate. Do not label a code as “sent” unless a verified delivery action actually occurred.
- Do not offer cash, discounts, status, access upgrades, or other incentives for issuing or redeeming invitations.

#### Measurement requirements

- Instrument or otherwise record the funnel only after explicit approval and privacy review.
- Measure code creation, optional share-method confirmation, landing, redemption, activation, and first useful member action as separate stages.
- Do not store recipient message content, contact-book data, or public identity. Use pseudonymous invitation/member identifiers.
- Do not infer “send” from “code created.” If the product has no delivery event, report send rate as **not measurable**, not zero.

### B. Proposed operating flow

#### 1. Audience and trigger

**Audience:** Existing members with fewer than three unclaimed codes.

**Recommended trigger:** After a member has completed a meaningful member action or explicitly opens Invitations—not immediately on sign-in and not as a blocking prompt.

**Recommended prompt:**

> **Know one person who would value a smaller, traceable city shortlist? You can prepare a personal invitation. Invite only someone you know; member identities and private activity stay private.**

**Hypothesis:** A relevance-and-trust prompt will produce better-fit recipients than generic “invite friends” language, without incentives.

#### 2. Member action

1. Member opens **Invitations**.
2. Surface shows **X of 3 invitation slots available** and lists unclaimed codes.
3. Member selects **Prepare a personal invitation**.
4. The server confirms code creation.
5. Only after confirmation, available slots change from X to X−1 because the new code is now unclaimed.
6. Member copies the code and recommended message into their own chosen private channel.

**Important semantic rule:** The current product does not establish a built-in send. Therefore, the system must not claim “Invitation sent” merely because a code was created or copied.

#### 3. Recipient message

**Recommended member-to-recipient copy:**

> I thought you might appreciate Detour: a private member community alongside a public map of exceptional tables already narrowed to city shortlists, with named recognition and official sources you can trace. I have a personal, one-time invitation for you. If you’d like to join, open Detour’s member page and use this code: **[CODE]**. No pressure—please don’t post or forward the code publicly.

**Short variant:**

> A personal Detour invitation for you: explore the public city shortlists, then use **[CODE]** on the member page if the private community feels relevant. Please keep the one-time code private.

Neither message claims that every place is member-recommended, that the inviter’s private activity will be visible, or that membership supplies booking or reservation access.

#### 4. Recipient landing path

**Recommended path:** Recipient opens the existing member route directly to **Use an invitation**, with a visible link back to public city discovery.

**Recommended landing copy:**

> **Join with your personal invitation.**  
> Detour pairs public, traceable city shortlists with a private member community. Enter your one-time code to create your membership.

**Supporting privacy line:**

> **Member identities, notes, and introductions remain private. Public selection follows a corroborated standard.**

**Unknown to preserve:** The public experience does not currently explain how a person without a member invitation can request one. Do not add a dead-end request CTA to campaign material.

#### 5. Redemption and activation

- Recipient enters the one-time code and required account details.
- On successful redemption, membership activates according to current behavior.
- Show a neutral confirmation:

> **Your membership is ready. Sign in to continue.**

- Do not expose the inviter’s private notes, activity, or other recipients.
- If redemption fails, use operational language such as “This invitation could not be accepted; check the code or ask the person who shared it,” without revealing whether another named person claimed it.

#### 6. Cap behavior

- Always show **remaining available slots** and **current unclaimed count**.
- At **0 available**, disable or hold new-code creation and explain:

> **You have three unclaimed invitations. A slot becomes available when one is redeemed.**

- Do not decrement a slot on button press, modal open, copy action, or an unverified external message.
- Under current actual semantics, a slot is occupied only after successful server-side code creation and returns only after successful redemption. If a future delivery system changes that behavior, update the copy and event model only after verifying the implementation.

#### 7. Quality and privacy rules

- Invite people the member knows; no purchased lists, scraping, bulk outreach, public code posting, or unsolicited mass messaging.
- No incentives or referral rewards.
- Do not reveal inviter or recipient identity publicly.
- Do not include private notes, introductions, recommendations, or activity in the invitation message.
- Do not imply selection influence, guaranteed publication, status, or editorial access.
- A code is one-time and personal; ask recipients not to forward it.
- Provide a way for a member to stop and leave the code unshared; do not pressure members to fill all three slots.

### C. Experiment variants

Run only after approval and only with enough members to avoid exposing individual behavior.

| Variant | Member prompt | Recipient emphasis | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Shortlist value | “Know one person who would value a smaller, traceable city shortlist?” | Public map and named-source proof first | **Hypothesis:** Clear public utility improves invitation comprehension. |
| B — Private standard | “Know one person who would value a private, corroborated community?” | Privacy and three-recommendation publication rule first | **Hypothesis:** Community quality framing improves fit but may reduce redemption if payoff remains abstract. |
| C — City relevance | “Know one person choosing restaurants in San Francisco?” | San Francisco’s 25-place map first, membership second | **Hypothesis:** A concrete city need outperforms general membership framing. |

Do not vary incentives, urgency, artificial scarcity, or public identity.

### D. Instrumentation event definitions

| Event | Trigger | Required properties | Privacy/semantic note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `invite_panel_viewed` | Signed-in member views Invitations | pseudonymous member ID, available slots, unclaimed count, timestamp | Never include codes. |
| `invite_create_started` | Member selects prepare/create | pseudonymous member ID, available slots, variant | Not a created or sent invite. |
| `invite_code_created` | Server confirms code record | pseudonymous member ID, pseudonymous invite ID, available slots after creation, variant | This is when the current slot is occupied. Never log the code value. |
| `invite_code_copied` | Member copies code/message | pseudonymous invite ID, variant | Diagnostic only; not proof of send. |
| `invite_share_method_confirmed` | Optional member-selected “I shared it” acknowledgement | pseudonymous invite ID, broad channel category | Self-reported; do not collect recipient address or message text. Omit if not approved. |
| `invite_landing_viewed` | Recipient opens invitation landing | pseudonymous invite/campaign token if safely available, source/medium | Do not expose inviter identity. |
| `invite_redemption_started` | Recipient submits join form | pseudonymous invite ID, timestamp | No password or raw code in analytics. |
| `invite_redeemed` | Server confirms claim | pseudonymous invite ID, pseudonymous inviter/member IDs, time-to-redeem | This is when the slot returns under current semantics. |
| `member_activated` | New membership becomes usable | pseudonymous new-member ID, invitation cohort | Keep separate if activation can ever differ from redemption. |
| `new_member_first_useful_action` | Pre-approved meaningful member action | pseudonymous new-member ID, action type, days since activation | Define before launch; do not expose private content. |
| `invite_create_blocked_cap` | Creation attempted/held at 0 available | pseudonymous member ID, unclaimed count | Diagnostic for cap comprehension. |

Core derived measures: code-created-to-redeemed rate, median time to redemption where sample permits, activation rate, first-useful-action rate, cap-block rate, and funnel loss by copy variant. “Sent invitations” must remain unavailable unless a real delivery or approved confirmation event exists.

## 4. Tester interview guide

**Status:** Proposed research plan only. Do not recruit or contact anyone without explicit user approval.

### Research objectives

Validate whether prospective users and newly activated members understand and value:

- a finite city shortlist rather than broad coverage;
- named-source traceability;
- the amount of information needed to choose among recognized places;
- the private member/public-selection distinction;
- invitation access and the three-unclaimed-invitation cap;
- the decided short-form message and its handoff to the city page.

### Proposed participant mix and quotas

**Target:** 12–16 moderated sessions, 25–35 minutes each.

| Cohort | Proposed quota | Mix |
|---|---:|---|
| San Francisco residents | 3–4 | At least two people who make local dining choices; mix of frequent and occasional special-occasion diners. |
| Bay Area suburbs | 3–4 | East Bay, Peninsula/South Bay, or North Bay; include people who travel into San Francisco and people who mostly dine locally. |
| Annecy / Haute-Savoie | 3–4 | Local residents; include at least two who plan restaurant choices for travel to larger cities. |
| Recent or likely visitors to a covered city | 3–4 | Mix across San Francisco, Paris, or Madrid where feasible. |

Within the total, aim for **8–12 prospective users** and **4 newly activated members**, if an approved and privacy-safe member sample exists. Do not create memberships solely to fill a quota. Seek a roughly even split between local-city and travel use cases. These are proposed quotas, not claims about market representation.

### Consent/opening script

> Thank you for helping us evaluate how clearly Detour explains itself. We are testing the product and language, not you. The current public app shows city maps and recognition sources; some member areas are invitation-only. Please think aloud. We will not ask you to reveal private notes, invitation codes, passwords, or other people’s identities. With your permission, we would like to take notes [and record the screen/audio only if separately approved]. You may stop at any time. Is that okay?

### Structured live task on the current public app

1. Start on the Detour public landing page without explanation.
2. Ask the participant to say what they believe Detour is and what they would use it for.
3. Give a scenario:
   - **Bay Area:** “You want to choose a recognized table in San Francisco for an upcoming meal, but you do not want to search an endless map.”
   - **Annecy:** “You are considering a future meal in Paris and want a credible starting set you can verify.”
4. Ask them to open the relevant city, use the map/list, select one place, find “Why it’s here,” and locate the official-source link.
5. Ask them to visit the Members route, explain what they think membership and invitations mean, and stop before entering any private credential or code.
6. Show one approved short-form draft/storyboard without posting it, then ask what promise they heard and what they expect after tapping through.

### Interview questions (10)

1. **First impression:** In your own words, what is Detour, and what job would you use it for?
2. **Shortlist value:** When you see “25 in San Francisco,” “29 in Paris,” or “43 in Madrid,” does the finite count feel usefully narrowed or insufficient? Why?
3. **Choice behavior:** Show me how you would use this page to get from the city to two places worth considering.
4. **Source trust:** What, if anything, makes you trust why a place is included? How do the named source, recognition level/year, and Official guide link affect that trust?
5. **Choice sufficiency:** After opening two places, do you have enough information to choose between them? What is missing, if anything—occasion, cuisine, neighborhood, price, a concise “why go,” or something else?
6. **Coverage context:** For Bay Area-suburb participants, when would a San Francisco shortlist be relevant versus a suburb-specific one? For Annecy participants, would you use this for Paris travel, and what would you expect from a future Annecy-area selection?
7. **Member comprehension:** After viewing Members, what do you believe members can do, and what remains private? Which parts are clear or unclear?
8. **Invitation comprehension:** How do you think a non-member obtains access? What do “personal invitation” and a maximum of three unclaimed invitations per member mean to you? What would you expect to happen when a code is created and when it is redeemed?
9. **Short-form-message comprehension:** After seeing the storyboard, what did “already narrowed to a credible, traceable city shortlist” mean to you? What did you expect the tap to open?
10. **Preference and action:** Which of these would most motivate you to open the city page: the exact city count, the map, the named recognition, the official-source link, or the private community standard? What would you do next, if anything?

### Moderator notes

- Ask for observed behavior before opinion.
- Do not teach the participant the intended answer.
- Mark each statement as direct quote, observed action, or moderator interpretation.
- Never request or record a real invitation code, password, private note, private share, or member identity.
- Do not promise an invitation, product change, city launch, partnership, or follow-up.
- Summarize findings by cohort and status (prospect/new member); do not generalize from this qualitative sample.

## 7. Measurement plan

### Hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis:** Organic San Francisco short-form content that demonstrates **finite count → city map → selected-place reason → named recognition/year → official source** will produce qualified use of the matching city page because it makes Detour’s shortlist and traceability legible before asking viewers to understand membership.

**Secondary hypothesis:** Count-first and proof-first creative will produce different downstream behavior; the stronger format can be identified through tracked city-page interactions rather than views or likes.

**Invitation hypothesis:** Interest may move from city use to the member route, but conversion will be constrained or difficult to interpret while the public flow lacks a clear invite-request route. This is an explicit unknown, not a failure to be hidden. [S1][S2]

### Scope and duration

**Recommended first test:** 14 consecutive days after approval, instrumentation QA, and link setup.

- **City:** San Francisco only for the primary test.
- **Creative:** Drafts 1–4, with at least one count-first and one proof-first format per approved platform where operationally feasible.
- **Platforms:** TikTok and Instagram Reels, using matching tracked links.
- **Destination:** The corresponding public San Francisco city page—not a generic homepage unless direct city linking is impossible and documented.
- **Paris/Madrid:** Hold as future sequence tests. Do not mix them into the first read.

### Clearly labeled starting assumptions

These are planning assumptions to verify before launch, not facts:

1. **Assumption:** A direct or reliably attributable San Francisco landing path can be used.
2. **Assumption:** Analytics can distinguish city-page entry, place open, proof/source interaction, and member-route entry, or a smaller approved proxy set.
3. **Assumption:** Each platform profile can expose a usable tracked link or approved link-in-bio path.
4. **Assumption:** Rights-cleared screen recordings can be produced from the current public app.
5. **Assumption:** Historical traffic may be too low or differently sourced to provide a stable conversion baseline.
6. **Assumption:** There is no clear public invitation-request route; therefore member-entry interest and completed invitation activation must not be treated as one continuous, fully available funnel.

### Minimal tracking and event taxonomy

| Event | Definition | Minimum properties |
|---|---|---|
| `gtm_link_clicked` | User follows an approved tracked social link | city, platform, creative ID, campaign, timestamp |
| `city_page_viewed` | Matching city page loads | city, platform/source where available, creative ID, session ID |
| `city_session_qualified` | Session completes the pre-approved meaningful-use rule | city, creative ID, qualifying action(s) |
| `place_opened` | Public place detail opens | city, anonymized place ID, creative ID |
| `proof_viewed` | “Why it’s here”/recognition proof is displayed or meaningfully engaged | city, anonymized place ID, source type, creative ID |
| `official_source_clicked` | User follows an Official guide link | city, anonymized place ID, source type, creative ID |
| `member_entry_viewed` | User opens Members | city/source session, creative ID |
| `invite_use_viewed` | User selects/views Use an invitation | creative/source session |
| `invite_request_attempted` | Only if a verified route exists later | route, source session | 
| `invite_redeemed` | Approved server-side confirmation of code redemption | pseudonymous invitation/cohort IDs |

**Qualified-session definition:** Approve one rule before launch and do not change it mid-test. Recommended rule: a city-page session with at least one meaningful product action—such as opening a place, using Refine/Show nearby, or viewing recognition proof—while excluding obvious bounces and internal QA traffic.

### Metrics

#### Primary

- **Qualified city-page sessions attributable to each creative.**
- **Qualified-session rate:** qualified city sessions ÷ tracked city-page sessions.

#### Secondary

- Place-open rate per city session.
- Proof-view rate per city session.
- Official-source click rate per city session.
- Member-entry rate per qualified city session.
- Use-an-invitation view rate per member-entry view.
- Invitation redemption/activation only where privacy-safe, instrumented, and attributable.

#### Diagnostic, not success

- Video views.
- Average watch time/completion where available.
- Likes.
- Comments.
- Shares on the social platform.
- Profile visits.
- Follows.
- Raw outbound clicks.

These diagnostics may explain creative delivery or message comprehension. **Views, likes, and follows are not experiment success.**

### Baseline process

1. Exclude team/internal QA traffic where possible.
2. Review the most recent comparable organic city-page traffic window available before launch, ideally seven days. Record source mix, city sessions, qualified-session rate, place-open rate, proof-view rate, official-source click rate, and member-entry rate.
3. If no usable history exists, state **“no reliable historical baseline”** and run a 48-hour instrumentation QA period with no external posting. Use the first approved creative wave as the initial benchmark; do not backfill invented baselines.
4. Record analytics coverage: percentage of tracked clicks that reconcile to sessions, missing event rates, timezone, attribution window, bot filtering, and known platform limitations.
5. Set conditional targets only after the baseline and expected sample are visible.

### Conditional targets

No performance result is forecast here. Before launch, the approver should fill in:

- **Primary target:** qualified-session rate at or above `[baseline/control + approved improvement threshold]`.
- **Proof target:** proof-view rate at or above `[baseline/control threshold]`.
- **Member-interest target:** member-entry rate that is `[at/above baseline or otherwise explicitly accepted]`, interpreted cautiously because invite-request access is unclear.
- **Data-quality target:** at least `[approved reconciliation threshold]` of tracked link activity attributable through the city session.
- **Minimum analyzable sample:** `[approved number of tracked city sessions/qualified sessions per creative]` based on actual baseline volume—not a fabricated universal benchmark.

### Cadence

**Daily, operational (10–15 minutes):**

- Confirm links and city destination work.
- Confirm events arrive and creative IDs are intact.
- Check factual comments/issues and hide or correct only under an approved moderation policy.
- Log spend as zero for organic unless that changes under separate approval.
- Record any rights, privacy, source, or invitation confusion; pause immediately on a guardrail breach.

**Twice during the 14 days:**

- Review watch/drop-off diagnostics and attributable city sessions.
- Do not optimize solely for reach. Preserve enough runtime for each approved creative to generate an interpretable comparison.

**Weekly:**

- Compare city/creative/platform cuts.
- Review qualified-session, proof interaction, official-source click, member-entry, and invitation-path observations.
- Document message comprehension from comments or approved interviews as qualitative evidence, not conversion data.

### Required analysis cuts

- **City:** San Francisco in the first test; later Paris and Madrid reported separately.
- **Creative:** count-first, proof-first, source-variety, known-place search.
- **Platform:** TikTok versus Instagram Reels.
- **Format/opening:** hook text, first three seconds, screen-record sequence.
- **Journey:** landing → qualified action → proof → source click → member entry → invitation use/redemption where available.
- **Audience context where ethically available:** local versus visitor intent, without collecting sensitive or unnecessary personal data.

### Continue / iterate / pause rule

Apply at day 14, or sooner for a safety/accuracy issue:

- **CONTINUE San Francisco and prepare the next approved wave** only if tracking is valid, the pre-approved minimum sample is reached, and at least two San Francisco creatives meet the conditional primary target with no factual/privacy breach. Continue the stronger proof sequence; Paris may be prepared only under a new posting approval.
- **ITERATE the hook or landing handoff** if attributable qualified sessions exist but the primary target is missed, only one creative works, proof interaction is weak, or member-entry behavior is uninterpretable because the invitation route is unclear. Keep the city fixed while changing one major variable at a time.
- **PAUSE external distribution** if tracking cannot support attribution, all approved San Francisco creatives complete their planned run without producing an analyzable qualified-session sample, the city-page handoff is broken, comments/interviews show the message systematically implies unsupported review/booking/invitation claims, or any privacy, rights, source-accuracy, or approval breach occurs.

Do not graduate a creative because it has the most views, likes, or follows.
